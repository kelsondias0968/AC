Why You Need AC

South African Summers Are Getting Hotter — Here's How to Beat the Heat

Average temperatures across South Africa have risen significantly over the past decade. Cities like Johannesburg, Cape Town, and Durban regularly see summer peaks above 35°C — making a reliable air conditioner no longer a luxury, but a necessity.

Beyond comfort, extreme heat affects sleep quality, productivity, and health. Studies show that sleeping in temperatures above 26°C can reduce deep sleep by up to 40%, leaving you exhausted the next day.

Signs you need an AC unit now

  • You wake up sweating during summer nights
  • Your home stays hot long after sunset
  • Fans no longer provide enough relief
  • You have children, elderly, or pets at home
  • Your electricity bill spikes from running multiple fans

Buying Guide

How to Choose the Right Air Conditioner for Your Space

Not all AC units are created equal. The wrong size unit will either struggle to cool the room or waste energy cycling on and off. Use this simple guide to make the right choice.

Room size vs recommended BTU

  • Up to 20m² — 9,000 BTU is sufficient
  • 20–35m² — go for 12,000 BTU
  • 35–50m² — 18,000 BTU recommended
  • 50m²+ — 24,000 BTU or split system

Key features to look for

  • Inverter technology — saves up to 40% on electricity
  • 5-star energy rating for long-term savings
  • Heating mode for winter use (heat pump)
  • WiFi/app control for remote scheduling
  • Sleep mode and quiet operation (below 30dB)
  • Auto-restart after power outages

A quality split AC unit with inverter tech typically pays for itself within 18–24 months compared to running portable coolers, especially given South Africa's energy prices.

Maintenance Tips

5 Simple Habits That Double the Life of Your AC Unit

Most AC units fail prematurely due to neglected maintenance. These five habits keep your system running efficiently for 10–15 years.

Monthly & seasonal checklist

  • Clean or replace filters every 4–6 weeks
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of debris and direct sun
  • Set temperature to 24°C — every degree lower uses 8% more power
  • Book a professional service once a year before summer
  • Use timer/sleep mode to avoid running overnight on full power
